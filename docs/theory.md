# Foundations of latency-adjusted predictive skill

This note separates an information bound, a measurable model score, and an
arrival-process approximation. They coincide only under the assumptions below.
The action-time target, event clock, retraining policy, and primary experimental
score follow [decisions.md](decisions.md). This is theory and an offline numerical
check, not a trained-model result or evidence of an FPGA implementation.

All numerical examples and table cells below trace to
[checks.json](../results/theory/checks.json), generated by
[theory_checks.py](../sims/theory_checks.py). JSON paths identify the relevant
objects. Supplied literature numbers are recorded separately as unverified
references; recording them does not independently substantiate them.

## Assumptions

- **Clock and observability.** An event includes messages changing the visible
  book or trades. Messages at an identical timestamp form one event, whose state
  is the last post-message book. Information at an anchor contains only its
  observed past. The local implementation compares all visible levels, retains
  trade types specified in the data rules, and excludes halt messages.
- **Labels and boundaries.** Best quotes are valid; L-next exists within the
  permitted split. Anchors whose future label is unavailable are excluded,
  rather than assigned a fabricated direction. Stationary propositions use an
  unbounded process without session censoring; finite-session estimates do not
  automatically satisfy that idealization.
- **Information theorem.** Expectations and the entropy baseline refer to the
  same evaluation population. The stationary theorem requires a strictly
  stationary, two-sided joint process and its complete, shift-equivariant past
  filtration. It does not apply automatically to a fixed-length feature window.
- **Scoring and estimation.** Predictive distributions have positive probability
  on labels that can occur and integrable logarithmic loss. Training, validation,
  calibration, and evaluation are temporally separated. A model's measured
  skill is a lower bound only after accounting for a mismatched entropy baseline
  and sampling uncertainty.
- **Monotone surrogate.** Isotonic fitting is a shape-constrained description of
  retrained-model skill under the stationary-information interpretation. It
  neither proves stationarity nor estimates unrestricted information without
  further consistency assumptions. Clipping negative fitted skill defines a
  nonnegative surrogate, not a better predictor.
- **Uncertainty.** A moving-block bootstrap assumes sufficiently weak dependence
  across blocks and local stability of the evaluation distribution. The adopted
  block length is a research-plan choice, not established here as sufficient.
  A percentile interval is an approximate uncertainty summary; regular behavior
  additionally needs a resolved crossing away from zero and projection boundaries.
  Model-training and calibration uncertainty require refitting if they are to
  be covered.
- **PGF conversion.** Replacing realized-delay scoring with marginal averaging
  assumes conditional mean skill at each delay equals its unconditional mean.
  Synthetic windows sample stationary calendar time; market diagnostics sample
  event anchors. These are different sampling populations for clustered arrivals.
- **Solvable model.** Initial skills are positive, event-time skill is exponential,
  and the simple crossover comparison uses equal decay factors. Homogeneous
  Poisson counts are independent of anchor informativeness. The Hawkes example
  is a stationary linear Poisson-cluster process with an exponential offspring
  kernel and subcritical branching; omitted prehistory has a quantified bound.
- **L-next approximation.** Survival scaling of skill assumes no useful signal
  on a replacement move, no dependence of surviving skill on waiting time, and
  an additive regime-wise score. Those conditions are stronger than persistence
  of the label. An unobserved mixture scored by a single probability forecast
  need not have skill proportional to survival.
- **Market plug-ins.** Mid-change frequency is measured after timestamp merging
  and trimming. Converting it to a geometric time constant assumes a constant
  event-time hazard. Session rates use the fixed session duration. Burst splits
  defined by future counts are retrospective diagnostics, not causal features.
  Count diagnostics use the whole trimmed session with complete latency windows;
  they precede model lookback, label-validity, and chronological-split filtering.
- **Hypothesis comparison.** The margin scenario equates fast initial skill to
  the slow skill times the retained-skill fraction. It is a boundary scenario
  for H1, not a measured quantization loss. The liquidity corollary holds other
  parameters, especially the event-time decay factor, fixed.
- **References and scope.** Literature descriptions are those supplied in
  [theory_spec.md](theory_spec.md); bibliographic details and source numbers are
  unverified offline. No network access, package installation, synthesis, remote
  execution, or empirical training is part of these checks.

## 1. Setup: information at the anchor, label after the action

Let $i$ index merged book events, $t_i$ their strictly increasing timestamps in
seconds, and $B_i,M_i$ their book states and message marks. For the stationary
theory, take $i\in\mathbb Z$ and define

$$
\mathcal F_i=\sigma(B_j,M_j,\text{observed inter-event gaps}:j\le i).
$$

The experiment observes the top $10$ visible levels plus trades
([checks.json](../results/theory/checks.json), *experiment_constants.visible_book_levels*).

Absolute calendar time can also be an observable in an experiment, but it
violates the shift-invariance used later unless its distribution is modeled
appropriately. Let $m_i$ be the valid mid-price after event $i$. Define

$$
K(j)=\inf\{k>j:m_k\ne m_j\},\qquad
Z_j=\operatorname{sign}(m_{K(j)}-m_j),\qquad
Y_{i,\tau}=Z_{i+\tau}.
$$

The label alphabet is up/down; there is no unchanged class in L-next. Crucially,
the reference price is $m_{i+\tau}$ and the labeled move occurs strictly after
the action event. Scoring a delayed model against $Z_i$ would reward a prediction
about a move that might already have happened.

A model is a conditional distribution $q_{m,\tau}(y\mid\mathcal F_i)$. The delay
subscript makes the adopted retrain-per-delay policy explicit. A train-once model
instead reuses $q_{m,0}$ at every delay; its misspecification can grow as labels
move forward. A causal feature vector is a measurable function of $\mathcal F_i$,
usually containing much less information than the whole filtration.

For wall-clock latency $L\ge0$, define the realized event delay

$$
D_i(L)=\tau_i(L)=N((t_i,t_i+L]).
$$

The open left endpoint excludes the anchor event and the closed right endpoint
includes an event exactly at the action deadline. The action label is
$Z_{i+D_i(L)}$. The action need not coincide with a message: this index refers
to the last observed book state by its deadline. Event counts are computed by
a right-sided timestamp search, rather than by rounding $\lambda L$.

Finite experiments must enforce split boundaries for both inputs and action
labels and use common anchors when comparing delays. Otherwise a change in
which anchors survive censoring can masquerade as information decay.

## 2. Skill as information, and the entropy-baseline qualification

Fix a delay and an evaluation population. With base-two logarithms, write

$$
\ell_{m,i}(\tau)=-\log_2q_{m,\tau}(Y_{i,\tau}\mid\mathcal F_i),\qquad
S_m(\tau)=H(Y_{i,\tau})-\mathbb E\ell_{m,i}(\tau).
$$

Skill has units of bits per prediction. Let
$p_\tau(y\mid\mathcal F_i)$ be the true conditional distribution. Adding and
subtracting its log probability gives the exact identity

$$
\mathbb E\ell_{m,i}(\tau)
=H(Y_{i,\tau}\mid\mathcal F_i)
+\mathbb E D_{\rm KL,2}
  (p_\tau(\cdot\mid\mathcal F_i)\Vert q_{m,\tau}(\cdot\mid\mathcal F_i)).
$$

Gibbs' inequality makes the expected divergence nonnegative. Consequently,

$$
S_m(\tau)
=I(Y_{i,\tau};\mathcal F_i)-\mathbb E D_{\rm KL,2}(p_\tau\Vert q_{m,\tau})
\le I^*(\tau):=I(Y_{i,\tau};\mathcal F_i).
$$

Equality holds exactly when the forecast equals the true conditional
distribution almost surely, including its probability mass on supported labels.
A forecast assigning zero probability to a possible label can have infinite
loss and skill unbounded below. The information ceiling itself is nonnegative.
Using a restricted feature vector further lowers the ceiling by data processing.

This population identity needs a qualification for the repository metric.
[decisions.md](decisions.md) uses the entropy of the *training* class prior:

$$
S_m^{\rm repo}(\tau)
=H(\pi_{\rm train,\tau})-\mathbb E_{\rm test}\ell_{m,i}(\tau).
$$

If the test label prior is $\pi_{\rm test,\tau}$, then

$$
S_m^{\rm repo}(\tau)
=I^*_{\rm test}(\tau)-\mathbb E_{\rm test}D_{\rm KL,2}(p_\tau\Vert q_{m,\tau})
+H(\pi_{\rm train,\tau})-H(\pi_{\rm test,\tau}).
$$

Thus the stated bound applies directly under a matched-prior assumption, or
after subtracting the entropy mismatch. The planned statistic is retained;
the qualification prevents calling its raw value an unconditional information
bound under distribution shift. Nor is entropy of the training prior necessarily
the test loss of a constant training-prior forecast. That baseline's test loss is
$H(\pi_{\rm test})+D_{\rm KL,2}(\pi_{\rm test}\Vert\pi_{\rm train})$.
Finite-sample skill can also exceed a population bound by sampling error.

Temperature scaling replaces logits $z_y$ with $z_y/T$ for validation-fitted
$T>0$. It adjusts probabilities and therefore logarithmic loss. It preserves
the within-anchor ordering of class logits and their argmax; for a binary model
with one global temperature it also preserves ranking of positive-class
probabilities across anchors. No blanket cross-anchor ranking claim is needed
for a multiclass model. A deterministic rescaling adds no input information,
cannot raise the information ceiling, and does not guarantee better test loss.
Calibration must use validation labels for the relevant delay or wall-clock
target, never the held-out labels used to report skill.

## 3. Monotonicity: a theorem about available information

**Proposition.** If the joint marked-state/label process is strictly stationary
in event time and $\mathcal F_i$ is its complete past filtration, then $I^*(\tau)$
is non-increasing for nonnegative integer delays.

**Proof.** Let $\tau'\ge\tau$ and $d=\tau'-\tau$. Stationarity allows shifting
both the target and the entire conditioning past by $d$ events:

$$
I(Z_{i+\tau'};\mathcal F_i)
=I(Z_{i+\tau};\mathcal F_{i-d}).
$$

Because $\mathcal F_{i-d}\subseteq\mathcal F_i$, conditional entropy cannot
increase on enlarging the conditioning sigma-field. Equivalently, data processing
for nested observations gives

$$
I(Z_{i+\tau};\mathcal F_{i-d})
\le I(Z_{i+\tau};\mathcal F_i)=I^*(\tau).
$$

This proves the assertion without assuming a Markov state, independent events,
or exponential decay. Stationarity is doing the work of keeping the target
distribution fixed while its accessible past recedes.

The theorem does **not** prove that every model's measured $S_m(\tau)$ decreases.
In the decomposition above, approximation/calibration divergence can change
with delay. An unrestricted Bayes-optimal model attains the bound; a restricted
model need not follow its shape. Similarly, two shifted fixed-length feature
windows need not be nested, even if the underlying market is stationary.

Practical sources of apparent nonmonotonicity include intraday seasonality,
different anchor populations after censoring, finite-sample noise, changing
training/test class mixes, and model misspecification. Horizon-dependent labels
such as FI-2010-style smoothed labels can also change the target functional and
its class mix, rather than simply shift the same $Z_j$. The proof then does not
apply. None of those mechanisms contradicts the proposition's stated conditions.

Before reading a decay half-life, use a non-increasing isotonic fit to the
retrained skill curve and report the raw curve beside it. A systematic,
well-resolved reversal calls the assumptions or model approximation into
question; projecting it away is not evidence that information was monotone.
The train-once curve remains a separate robustness result.

## 4. Half-life and an uncertainty estimator

Let $0=\tau_0<\cdots<\tau_J$ be the tested delay grid and $s_j$ its estimated
skills. Fit

$$
(\widetilde s_j)_{j=0}^J
=\arg\min_{u_0\ge\cdots\ge u_J}\sum_{j=0}^J w_j(s_j-u_j)^2,\qquad
\widehat I_j=\max(0,\widetilde s_j).
$$

Weights must be chosen before inspecting the desired crossing; equal weights
are a simple default. Pool-adjacent-violators solves this least-squares problem.
Its nonnegative projection is also the constrained solution with $u_J\ge0$.
The notation $\widehat I$ denotes a skill-based information *surrogate*; a lower
bound curve does not identify the half-life of the actual $I^*$.

Define the half-life by the first crossing of half the fitted initial value,
using piecewise-linear interpolation:

$$
\widehat\tau_{1/2}
=\inf\{\tau:\widehat I(\tau)\le\widehat I(0)/2\}.
$$

For a crossing in $[\tau_{j-1},\tau_j]$, interpolate the endpoints:

$$
\widehat\tau_{1/2}
=\tau_{j-1}+(\tau_j-\tau_{j-1})
\frac{\widehat I_{j-1}-\widehat I_0/2}
      {\widehat I_{j-1}-\widehat I_j}.
$$

If initial fitted skill is nonpositive, the half-life is unidentified. If no
crossing occurs within the grid, report a right-censored lower bound beyond the
last delay. An exponential extrapolation would introduce an additional
assumption. A plateau at the target uses its first crossing.

The executable example pools the reversed adjacent skills into $0.31$ bits,
clips the negative endpoint to zero, and gives a half-life of $3.047619$ events.
Its inputs, fit and output are in
[checks.json](../results/theory/checks.json), *analytic_examples.half_life_example*.
This checks the estimator, not a market half-life.

For an interval, resample contiguous time blocks of held-out anchors jointly
across every delay. Recompute the prior convention, mean losses, isotonic fit,
and crossing within each replicate. Use the adopted $300$-second blocks,
$1000$ replicates, seed $0$, and a $95\%$ percentile interval
([checks.json](../results/theory/checks.json), *experiment_constants*;
[gate0_spec.md](gate0_spec.md)). Under the stated bootstrap assumptions, the
empirical lower and upper tail quantiles estimate uncertainty in this fitted
model's half-life. Coverage is not established here, especially for flat,
tied, or nearly zero curves where the projection/crossing is nonregular.
If substantial replicate mass is right-censored, retain an
unbounded endpoint and report that mass; do not discard failed crossings.
Also report the fraction with no positive initial skill. The bootstrap is
conditional on the fitted models unless training and calibration are refitted.

Raw accuracy is not a logarithmic skill measure. Under no useful signal, its
baseline is the majority-class probability, which is $0.5$ only for balanced
labels. An exponential fit to raw accuracy spuriously drives it toward zero;
subtracting an accuracy floor still does not turn it into information. Fit
the defined skill surrogate, with its prior and calibration explicitly recorded.

## 5. Event time to wall-clock: marginal averaging and selection

Write $b_\tau$ for the chosen population entropy baseline and define per-anchor
skill contributions

$$
h_{m,i}(\tau)=b_\tau-\ell_{m,i}(\tau),\qquad
S_m(\tau)=\mathbb E h_{m,i}(\tau).
$$

For a delay-indexed family of forecasts, the score actually experienced by
anchors at latency $L$ is

$$
B_m(L)=\mathbb E[h_{m,i}(D_i(L))]
=\sum_\tau P(D=\tau)\,
  \mathbb E[h_{m,i}(\tau)\mid D=\tau].
$$

The marginal conversion in the research decisions is instead

$$
A_m(L)=\sum_\tau P(D=\tau)S_m(\tau).
$$

Its exact gap is

$$
B_m(L)-A_m(L)
=\sum_\tau\operatorname{Cov}
  \big(h_{m,i}(\tau),\mathbf1\{D_i(L)=\tau\}\big).
$$

**Derivation.** Expand $B$ with the mutually exclusive delay indicators and
subtract $\sum_\tau\mathbb E[h(\tau)]\mathbb E[\mathbf1\{D=\tau\}]$ term by term.
The identity is equally exact for finite-anchor averages if empirical
covariances use the population denominator. For delay-dependent baselines it
uses those same $b_\tau$ in both scores; a different wall-clock prior adds a
separate baseline correction.

This is a covariance with the *delay selection indicators*, not generally one
scalar $\operatorname{Cov}(h,D)$. Merely saying that informative moments coincide
with bursts does not determine its sign, because skill at the selected future
target may have either increased or decreased.

For a precise sufficient condition, suppose
$h_i(\tau)=a_i r^\tau$, $a_i\ge0$, and $0<r<1$. Then

$$
B(L)-A(L)=\operatorname{Cov}(a_i,r^{D_i(L)}).
$$

If $\mathbb E[a_i\mid D=d]$ increases with $d$, this covariance is nonpositive:
for independent copies $D,D'$, the product of the differences of an increasing
function and $r^d$, a decreasing function, is nonpositive, and its expectation
is twice their covariance. Under this explicit meaning of “informative moments
coincide with bursts,” marginal averaging overstates retained skill. Without
the separable decay and ordering assumptions, the sign is undetermined.

Following the recorded research plan, $A_m(L)$ remains the primary cross-delay
summary and Gate 0 verdict statistic: it exposes separately measured event-time
skill and the event-count distribution on a common anchor population.
$B_m(L)$ is the required check on whether that factorization represents
action-time performance. A discrepancy limits any operational interpretation
of the marginal score and must be reported, rather than absorbed into the
Poisson approximation or silently changing the preregistered verdict.

There is one further distinction. Direct retraining on wall-clock labels learns
$q_{m,L}$, while $B$ above selects $q_{m,\tau}$ after the realized count is known.
Future counts cannot be used to select a deployed forecast at the anchor.
Therefore the direct fixed-$L$ model is the implementable performance check.
Its difference from $A$ includes model-family and calibration differences as
well as the covariance and any entropy-baseline mismatch. Agreement is useful;
disagreement is not a pure estimate of the covariance unless forecasts and
baselines are held identical.

## 6. A solvable arrival/decay model

### Exponential event-time skill and the PGF

Assume

$$
S_m(\tau)=S_m(0)\rho_m^\tau,\qquad
\rho_m=\exp(-1/\tau_{c,m}),\quad 0<\rho_m<1.
$$

Here $\tau_c$ is an e-folding constant in events, not the half-life. Summing
over counts gives, under the marginal conversion assumptions,

$$
A_m(L)=S_m(0)\mathbb E[\rho_m^{D(L)}]
=S_m(0)G_L(\rho_m),\qquad G_L(z)=\mathbb E[z^{D(L)}].
$$

This first identity needs no Poisson model. It is a probability-generating
function evaluated below unity, rather than the substitution
$S_m(\lambda L)$. In particular, counts of zero preserve the whole initial
skill in this abstraction. The fitted information surrogate would have
event-time half-life $\tau_c\ln2$ if the exponential assumption held.

For homogeneous Poisson arrivals with rate $\lambda$ events per second,
$D(L)\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda L)$, so

$$
G_L(\rho)
=e^{-\lambda L}\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{(\lambda L\rho)^k}{k!}
=\exp[-\lambda L(1-\rho)].
$$

The numerical check uses synthetic $\lambda=20000$/s, $\rho=0.8$, and
$200000$ independent trials with root seed $20261013$. At $L=50$ microseconds,
the closed PGF is $0.818730753$ and Monte Carlo gives $0.819016723$ with
standard error $0.000369731$. The relative error is $0.034928\%$, below the
required $1\%$; every evaluated length passes.
These are synthetic process parameters, not estimated ticker parameters
([checks.json](../results/theory/checks.json), *seed*,
*arrivals.parameters*, and *arrivals.poisson_pgf.rows*).

### Crossover and the liquidity corollary

Let $L_f<L_s$, positive initial skills $S_f(0),S_s(0)$, and common $\rho$.
The Poisson score ratio is

$$
\frac{A_f(L_f)}{A_s(L_s)}
=\frac{S_f(0)}{S_s(0)}
\exp[\lambda(1-\rho)(L_s-L_f)].
$$

Taking natural logarithms proves that the fast model wins exactly when

$$
\lambda(1-\rho)\Delta L>
\ln\frac{S_s(0)}{S_f(0)},\qquad \Delta L=L_s-L_f.
$$

Equality is the crossover. When the slow model has strictly higher initial
skill, its critical latency advantage is

$$
\Delta L_{\rm crit}
=\frac{\ln[S_s(0)/S_f(0)]}{\lambda(1-\rho)}.
$$

Holding initial-skill ratio and decay fixed, this gap scales as $1/\lambda$.
That is H3's conditional liquidity corollary. If the fast model already has
at least as much initial skill, the positive-gap crossover interpretation
changes: it already wins at equal latency. If decay factors differ, use the
general log ratio

$$
\ln\frac{A_f(L_f)}{A_s(L_s)}
=\ln\frac{S_f(0)}{S_s(0)}
-\lambda(1-\rho_f)L_f+\lambda(1-\rho_s)L_s;
$$

there is no common-decay threshold without that restriction.

The brute-force check simulates coupled Poisson counts across a latency-gap
grid, rather than evaluating the closed expression as its search objective.
With the recorded margin ratio and synthetic arrival parameters, the analytic
gap is $26.340129$ microseconds. The simulated first-crossing bracket is
$[26.25,26.50]$ microseconds; interpolation gives $26.405752$ microseconds.
The gap error is $0.065623$ microseconds, versus the recorded tolerance of
$0.658253$ microseconds, comprising grid resolution and Monte Carlo uncertainty
([checks.json](../results/theory/checks.json), *crossover*).

### Shared overhead: when cancellation holds

Let model-only latencies be $\ell_f,\ell_s$ and shared pipeline overhead $c$,
so $L_m=\ell_m+c$. Independent stationary Poisson increments give

$$
G_{\ell+c}(\rho)=G_\ell(\rho)G_c(\rho).
$$

For **common** $\rho$, the factor $G_c$ cancels from $A_f/A_s$. It still lowers
both absolute scores. Different $\rho_m$ leave a factor
$\exp[\lambda c(\rho_f-\rho_s)]$, even for Poisson arrivals. Thus cancellation
requires both an arrival factorization and a common exponential decay law.

Non-Poisson counts generally lack the required multiplicative semigroup:
counts in adjacent windows may be dependent. A two-timescale skill curve also
breaks cancellation even with Poisson arrivals. For common mixture weights
and distinct decay factors,

$$
S_m(\tau)=S_m(0)[w\rho_1^\tau+(1-w)\rho_2^\tau],
$$

$$
A_m(L)=S_m(0)\left[
w e^{-\lambda(1-\rho_1)L}+(1-w)e^{-\lambda(1-\rho_2)L}\right].
$$

The overhead reweights those components, changing the comparison unless a
component is absent, the decay rates coincide, or another special cancellation
occurs. An equal-weight example using the recorded short and long time constants
changes the Poisson fast/slow ratio from $0.986486$ at zero overhead to
$0.980763$ at $40$ microseconds
([checks.json](../results/theory/checks.json),
*analytic_examples.two_timescale_poisson*).

The arrival simulation compares a Poisson process with a linear Hawkes process
of equal target rate. Its offspring kernel is
$\phi(u)=n\beta e^{-\beta u}$ for $u>0$, with $n=0.7$ and
$\beta=200000$/s; immigrant rate is $\lambda(1-n)$.
Each independent window includes $400$ microseconds of prehistory.
For maximum window length $L_{\max}$, the expected omitted count is bounded by

$$
\frac{\lambda n}{\beta(1-n)}
e^{-\beta(1-n)B}
\left(1-e^{-\beta(1-n)L_{\max}}\right),
$$

where $B$ is the burn interval. This follows by integrating the exponential
mean offspring response from immigrants preceding $-B$. The bound is
$8.77\times10^{-12}$ events per trial, or $1.75\times10^{-6}$ events across
all trials. The simulation samples calendar-time windows, not event-conditioned
Hawkes windows
([checks.json](../results/theory/checks.json), *arrivals.hawkes_parameters*).

The model-only latencies are $0.1$ and $50$ microseconds, and the common
exponential factor and initial skills are those recorded above. Ratios below

non-cancellation in this specified example. This is not a theorem that every
non-Poisson process has a practically significant overhead effect. It explains
why the adopted overhead sweep must be retained for real counts and fitted skill.

### Overdispersion, and why burst informativeness matters

For $0<\rho<1$, $x\mapsto\rho^x$ is convex:
its second derivative is $(\ln\rho)^2\rho^x>0$. Therefore if count $D_2$
dominates $D_1$ in **convex order** at the same mean, then

$$
\mathbb E[\rho^{D_2}]\ge\mathbb E[\rho^{D_1}].
$$

Higher variance alone does not establish convex order. The result applies to
a mean-preserving spread, and says that dispersion reduces the marginal
latency cost in this exponential, count-independent abstraction: extra
zero-count windows can outweigh extremely busy windows where little skill
survives.

Stationary linear Hawkes counts have a stronger argument for this particular
comparison. They are sums of independent immigrant-cluster contributions
$C_u$ to a fixed calendar window. The Poisson-cluster generating functional is

$$
\ln G_H(\rho)
=\mu\int_{\mathbb R}(\mathbb E[\rho^{C_u}]-1)\,du.
$$

This follows by partitioning immigrant time into independent Poisson cells:
each cell contributes its compound-Poisson log PGF, and their sum converges to
the integral. Subcritical branching makes the expected window count finite.

The Bernoulli inequality $\rho^k\ge1-k(1-\rho)$ for integer $k\ge0$ yields

$$
\ln G_H(\rho)\ge
-(1-\rho)\mu\int_{\mathbb R}\mathbb E[C_u]\,du
=-(1-\rho)\lambda L.
$$

Thus its PGF is at least the equal-mean Poisson PGF; it is strictly larger when
a cluster can contribute multiple window events with positive probability.
This proof is specific to the stationary Poisson-cluster construction and
calendar-time sampling.

At the recorded comparison window, Poisson and Hawkes have theoretical mean
count $1$. Simulated means are $0.998325$ and $1.010600$, respectively; count
variances are $0.999087$ and $8.086008$. Their simulated PGFs are $0.819017$
and $0.877176$. The PGF difference is $0.058159$, with standard error
$0.000638138$, and the Hawkes mean is within the recorded sampling tolerance
of its target
([checks.json](../results/theory/checks.json), *arrivals.overdispersion*).

This fixed-mean conclusion can reverse for *skill-weighted* windows. If large
counts select the most informative anchors, the negative covariance proved
above can offset or dominate the increase in the unweighted PGF. Moreover,
conditioning on an event changes the count distribution itself. Neither
calendar-time overdispersion nor a daily average rate suffices to characterize
the delay distribution seen by prediction anchors.

### L-next persistence and the geometric approximation

Let $T_i=K(i)-i$ be the number of events until the next mid change. If
$T_i>\tau$, no mid change has occurred in $(i,i+\tau]$, so the action label
equals the anchor label:

$$
Y_{i,\tau}=Z_i\quad\text{on }\{T_i>\tau\}.
$$

If $T_i\le\tau$, that equality is no longer guaranteed; the old move is already
past and the target is a later move. This explains why label survival is a
natural candidate timescale, but does not by itself prove exponential skill.

To obtain the proposed approximation, assume the expected contribution on a
surviving label is $S(0)$ regardless of $T_i$, and the expected contribution
on a replacement label is zero. Under this *additive regime-wise score*
assumption, conditioning on survival gives

$$
S(\tau)\simeq S(0)P(T_i>\tau).
$$

It is exact if those score assumptions hold exactly. They effectively require
the replacement regime to contribute prior-level logarithmic loss; merely
having no information about the replacement label does not guarantee that
an unchanged, confident forecast achieves that loss.

If the next-change hazard is constant per event,
$T$ is geometric on the positive integers:

$$
P(T>\tau)=(1-p)^\tau,\qquad
\rho=1-p,\qquad \tau_c=-1/\ln(1-p).
$$

Thus geometric *survival* is exactly exponential. Skill has that same factor
only under the score assumption; for small $p$, $\tau_c$ is approximately
$1/p$, but these quantities are not interchangeable.

An explicit probability-forecast counterexample shows the distinction.
Take a fair binary target initially predicted with accuracy $0.9$ from an
observable signal. Independently, the target survives with probability $0.5$;
otherwise replace it with an independent fair direction. The Bayes forecast's
new accuracy is $0.7$. Its information is $0.118709$ bits, whereas survival
times initial information is $0.265502$ bits. The old initial information was
$0.531004$ bits. Mixing probability forecasts and then taking log loss is
nonlinear
([checks.json](../results/theory/checks.json),
*analytic_examples.hidden_survival_example*). Exponential information decay
is consequently a falsifiable model assumption.

requires the appropriate skill-weighted count distribution and a comparison
at both models' latencies.

**Falsifiable Gate 0 prediction (conjecture).** Under homogeneous mean-rate
conversion, the stated margin scenario predicts no fast/slow crossover at the
proposed latency gap. On real event anchors, bursts can cause greater skill
loss than that conversion predicts, especially when they carry the signal.
Gate 0 tests the latter mechanism by estimating event-time skill, the full
realized count distribution, burst-separated skill, and direct wall-clock
retraining. If the primary fast-versus-delayed score difference is not resolved
on the required ticker population, the proposed latency range has not shown
measurable predictive cost on these samples.

The recorded Gate 0 rule compares the selected model at $100$ ns versus
$2$ ms and requires a $95\%$ interval excluding zero for at least $2$ of
$5$ tickers
([checks.json](../results/theory/checks.json), *experiment_constants*;
[gate0_spec.md](gate0_spec.md)). That evaluates H2 and the plausibility of H3's
mechanism. It does not itself compare a trained ternary model with a slow model
or verify H1/H3. No Gate 0 verdict is produced by these theory checks.

## 7. Relation to the supplied prior work

**Moallemi & Sağlam.** The specification supplies the year $2013$ and describes
a martingale-price, no-signal setting in which latency cost is execution risk
([checks.json](../results/theory/checks.json), *supplied_literature*;
[theory_spec.md](theory_spec.md)). Source details and that characterization are
**unverified offline**. Under an additional no-directional-signal assumption,
$P(Y\mid\mathcal F_i)=P(Y)$, the Bayes skill is zero by the information identity;
latency can nevertheless harm execution prices, fills, and inventory outcomes,
which this prediction score does not model. A martingale assumption alone
only constrains conditional *mean price changes* and does not imply zero
information about their sign. The executable counterexample has two equally
likely observable regimes with zero conditional mean increment in both:
up probability $0.9$ with increments $+1,-9$, and up probability $0.1$ with
increments $+9,-1$. Directional information is nevertheless $0.531004$ bits
([checks.json](../results/theory/checks.json),
*analytic_examples.martingale_direction_counterexample*).
This framework adds a conditional-information target and distinguishes lost
prediction skill from execution-only latency costs; it does not attribute
the extra directional assumptions to the paper without checking it.

**Aït-Sahalia, Fan, Xue & Zhou.** The supplied comparison is calendar-time
delayed-feature $R^2$ for INTC, falling from $14\%$ to $2.5\%$ after a
$10$ ms feature delay at a $5$ s prediction horizon
([checks.json](../results/theory/checks.json),
*supplied_literature.ait_sahalia_fan_xue_zhou*;
[theory_spec.md](theory_spec.md)). The source number, article title, year,
and exact protocol are **to verify**; the percentages are not freshly verified
results. The present framework adds an action-time directional label, calibrated
logarithmic skill in bits, and an explicit event-count conversion with a
selection-covariance qualification. An $R^2$ percentage cannot be converted
to those bits or to an event half-life without a joint predictive distribution
and the precise target definition.

**Lucchese, Pakkanen & Veraart.** The supplied year is $2024$, with an event-clock
framing and “predictability as a function of $\lambda^+$” identified as an open
direction
([checks.json](../results/theory/checks.json), *supplied_literature*;
[theory_spec.md](theory_spec.md)). Bibliographic details and that wording are
**to verify**; no definition of their $\lambda^+$ is invented here. This note
adds a stationary-filtration monotonicity argument, a calibrated information
surrogate, and a PGF-based conditional liquidity prediction with explicitly
testable arrival and signal-selection assumptions. It does not assert that
the supplied open direction was absent from every other treatment.

## 8. Predictions and refutation criteria

The margins and experimental constants in this table are recorded in
[checks.json](../results/theory/checks.json), *h1_tables* and
*experiment_constants*. These are hypotheses about future empirical work,
separate from the executable arithmetic checks.

| Hypothesis | Quantity | Framework prediction | Observation that would refute it |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1: small quantization loss | Paired $S_f(0)-S_s(0)$ relative to positive $S_s(0)$ | The fast model retains at least the adopted $90\%$ of slow-model initial skill, after delay-specific validation calibration. This is a modeling hypothesis, not implied by the information bound. | A paired interval resolving retention below that margin on the prespecified population. If slow skill is nonpositive, the relative-margin test is undefined. |
| H2: measurable delay decay | Retrained $S_m(\tau)$ and paired $A_m(100\text{ ns})-A_m(2\text{ ms})$ | Available stationary full-past information cannot increase; accessible model skill is expected to fall enough to resolve the Gate 0 contrast. | The adopted Gate 0 contrast fails its ticker criterion: measurable predictive cost at those latencies is not established on that day. A reproducible information increase under all theorem assumptions would challenge the theorem, while a model-score increase alone does not. |
| H3: fast model crosses slow | Paired $A_f(\ell_f+c)-A_s(\ell_s+c)$, validated by direct action-time scoring | With common exponential decay and appropriate Poisson counts, a positive crossover occurs at the derived gap; real-data selection can change it. | A resolved slow-model advantage beyond the predicted threshold while the initial-skill ratio, common decay, arrival distribution, and conditional-independence assumptions are independently supported. If those assumptions fail, the solvable model is refuted, rather than universal H3. |
| Liquidity corollary | $\lambda\Delta L_{\rm crit}$ at fixed skill ratio and $\rho$ | The product is constant in the equal-decay Poisson model; increasing the rate decreases the critical gap. | A resolved failure of inverse-rate scaling in matched regimes with the other parameters held fixed. Different-ticker comparisons with changing $\rho$ do not supply that control. |
