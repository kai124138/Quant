# Theory spec: foundations of latency-adjusted predictive skill

Write `docs/theory.md` (Markdown with LaTeX in `$...$` / `$$...$$`) and
`sims/theory_checks.py` (numerical checks, fixed seeds, runs in under a minute with
`.venv/bin/python sims/theory_checks.py`, writes `results/theory/checks.json`).
Read `docs/decisions.md` first. Rigor over length: every claim is either proved, cited, or
labelled a conjecture. State assumptions explicitly. Target 8-12 pages.

## Sections and the results each must contain

1. **Setup.** Event clock (index i, times t_i), filtration F_i generated by book states and
   messages up to event i, action-time label Y_{i,tau} (L-next: direction of the first mid
   change after event i + tau). Models as conditional distributions q_m(y | F_i). Define
   tau_i(L) = N((t_i, t_i + L]).

2. **Skill as information.** Define S_m(tau) = H(Y) - E[-log2 q_m(Y_{i,tau} | F_i)].
   Prove S_m(tau) <= I(Y_{i,tau}; F_i) =: I*(tau) (cross-entropy >= conditional entropy, Gibbs),
   with equality iff q_m is the true conditional. So measured skill is a lower bound on the
   information still available at delay tau. Discuss what temperature scaling does and does
   not change (calibration vs. ranking).

3. **Monotonicity.** Prove: if the joint process of states and labels is strictly stationary
   in event time, then I*(tau) is non-increasing in tau (shift by tau'-tau, then data
   processing on F_{i-(tau'-tau)} subset of F_i). List what breaks monotonicity in practice:
   intraday seasonality, horizon-dependent class mix (the FI-2010 label), finite samples,
   model misspecification. Consequence: fit an isotonic (non-increasing) curve before
   reading a half-life.

4. **Half-life.** Define tau_1/2 by I-hat(tau_1/2) = I-hat(0)/2 on the isotonic fit, with
   floor zero because S is measured relative to the prior. Give an estimator and a
   bootstrap interval; explain why fitting an exponential to raw accuracy (floor 0.5 or the
   majority-class rate) is wrong.

5. **Event time to wall-clock.** Define the per-anchor latency-adjusted score (each anchor
   scored at its realized tau_i(L)) and the conversion A_m(L) = sum_tau P(tau | L) S_m(tau).
   Derive the gap between them as a covariance between per-anchor skill and the realized
   delay, and state its sign under "informative moments coincide with bursts". Say which
   of the two the experiment should treat as primary and why the other is a check.

6. **A solvable model.** Assume S_m(tau) = S_m(0) rho_m^tau, rho = exp(-1/tau_c).
   (a) Show A_m(L) = S_m(0) G_L(rho_m), G_L the probability generating function of tau(L).
   (b) Poisson arrivals at rate lambda: G_L(rho) = exp(-lambda L (1 - rho)). Derive the
       crossover: fast model f beats slow model s iff
       lambda (1 - rho)(L_s - L_f) > ln(S_s(0) / S_f(0)) (equal rho). Show the critical
       wall-clock latency gap scales as 1/lambda (H3's liquidity corollary).
   (c) Shared overhead c: under Poisson, G_{l+c} = G_l G_c, so c cancels from the
       comparison. Show when it stops cancelling (non-Poisson arrivals, non-exponential
       decay such as a two-timescale mixture) and what that implies for the c sweep.
   (d) Overdispersion: for a fixed mean count, by convexity of rho^tau, more dispersed
       counts give larger G_L(rho). Show what that means for latency cost, and how
       correlation between bursts and informativeness can reverse it.
   (f) Why exponential decay is natural for L-next: if no mid change occurs in (i, i + tau],
       the label at i + tau equals the label at i. With T = events until the next mid
       change, derive S(tau) ~ S(0) P(T > tau) under stated assumptions (no skill on the
       later move, skill not dependent on T), and show geometric T gives exactly
       rho = 1 - p, p = mid changes per event. Measure p (events per mid change) for each
       ticker from `data/raw/lobster/` and use the measured tau_c in (e), alongside the
       {5, 20, 100} sweep.
   (e) Plug in H1's margin: S_s(0)/S_f(0) = 1/(1 - delta_1), delta_1 = 0.10, and give the
       required lambda (1 - rho) Delta L. Evaluate it with the measured mean rates of the
       2012 LOBSTER samples (AAPL 17.1/s, MSFT 28.6/s, INTC 26.7/s, AMZN 11.5/s,
       GOOG 6.3/s; median inter-event gaps 639, 51, 66, 620, 830 us) for Delta L = 2 ms and
       tau_c in {5, 20, 100} events. Show that mean-rate (Poisson) conversion predicts no
       crossover in most cells, while burst-conditioned rates can. Make this an explicit,
       falsifiable prediction that Gate 0 tests.

7. **Relation to prior work.** Moallemi & Sağlam 2013 (martingale price: no signal, so
   S = 0 and latency cost is purely execution risk); Ait-Sahalia, Fan, Xue & Zhou
   (calendar-time delayed-feature R^2, 14% to 2.5% after 10 ms for INTC at 5 s); Lucchese,
   Pakkanen & Veraart 2024 (event clock; "predictability as a function of lambda+" left open).
   One paragraph each on what this framework adds. Do not invent numbers or citations beyond
   these; mark anything else "to verify".

8. **Predictions table.** For H1, H2, H3 and the liquidity corollary: the quantity, what
   the framework predicts, and the observation that would refute it.
